# Audience Message Adapter

## Overview

The same facts land differently depending on who is reading them. This skill translates business information into the right voice, structure, and register for each audience — without changing the substance or compromising honesty.

Core principle: Adapt the delivery, never the truth. Every version must be accurate, humble, and respectful.

## Language Default

Default to English unless the context clearly requires otherwise (e.g., a letter to a Brazilian government agency, or a message to a Spanish-speaking franchisee who has indicated preference). When in doubt, ask.

Language priority: English first, Chinese second, Spanish third.

## Four Core Audience Profiles

### 1. HQ / Senior Leadership (Internal, Chinese context)

**What they care about:** Results, risks, resource needs. They have limited time and high information density.

**Structure:** Conclusion first, supporting facts second, risks third, ask last.

**Tone:** Factual, direct, modest. Do not oversell. Do not hide problems — surface them with solutions attached.

**Format:** Short paragraphs or bullet points. One page if possible. Numbers where available.

**What to avoid:** Vague optimism, promises without data, burying the key point in paragraph three.

```
Example structure:
[Market / Topic]: [One-line status]
Key result: [What happened, with number]
Risk or issue: [What is not going well + proposed solution]
Request: [What you need from them, specifically]
```

### 2. Prospective or Existing Franchisee

**What they care about:** Can I make money? Will the brand support me? Is this worth the risk?

**Structure:** Lead with the opportunity and support, follow with requirements and obligations, close with next step.

**Tone:** Warm, confident, grounded. Honest about challenges. Never oversell the financial projections.

**Format:** Clear sections, plain language, no jargon. Visual aids help (charts, tables).

**What to avoid:** Exaggerated income claims, vague promises of "full support," dismissing their concerns.

```
Example structure:
[Opening]: Acknowledge their interest and thank them genuinely
[Opportunity]: What makes this market / location interesting (with data)
[Support]: What the Americas BD department provides, specifically
[Requirements]: What is expected of the franchisee, clearly stated
[Next step]: One clear action
```

### 3. Legal Counsel

**What they care about:** Facts, documents, precise questions, timeline.

**Structure:** Context → specific legal question → relevant documents attached → deadline.

**Tone:** Professional, precise, efficient. Do not ask vague questions. Do not include unnecessary background.

**Format:** Short email or memo. Numbered questions if multiple. Attach all relevant documents upfront.

**What to avoid:** Emotional language, speculative statements, asking "what do you think generally?" without a specific question.

```
Example structure:
Context: [One paragraph — who, what, where, when]
Question(s): [Numbered list of specific legal questions]
Attached: [List of documents]
Needed by: [Specific date]
```

### 4. Government Agency or Regulator

**What they care about:** Compliance, clarity, proper process. They want to see that you have done your homework.

**Structure:** Purpose of the communication → factual description → request or action → contact information.

**Tone:** Formal, respectful, patient. Never confrontational. Acknowledge their authority.

**Format:** Formal letter format. Avoid casual language entirely. Use the agency's own terminology where possible.

**What to avoid:** Pushing back on process, expressing frustration, making statements that could be interpreted as non-compliance.

```
Example structure:
[Salutation and reference]: Dear [Title + Name], Re: [Subject with case or file number if available]
[Purpose]: We are writing to [register / inquire / submit] regarding...
[Facts]: [Factual description, dates, entities involved]
[Request]: We respectfully request [specific action]
[Contact]: For questions, please contact [name, title, email, phone]
```

## Adaptation Checklist

Before sending any communication, verify:

- [ ] Do I know who the primary reader is?
- [ ] Have I put the most important information first for this audience?
- [ ] Is the tone appropriate — not too casual, not too stiff?
- [ ] Have I removed jargon the reader would not understand?
- [ ] Does every claim reflect the truth — no exaggeration, no omission of material risk?
- [ ] Is the language humble and respectful throughout?
- [ ] Is there one clear next step or ask?
- [ ] Is English the right language, or should I ask Win?

## Same Message, Four Versions — Example

**Situation:** A key supplier has delayed the shipment of branded cups by three weeks, affecting two store openings.

**HQ version (Chinese context, internal):**
> Two store openings in [City A] and [City B] will be delayed by approximately three weeks due to a branded packaging shipment delay from our supplier. Root cause is a customs clearance issue at the port of origin. The Americas BD department is pursuing two mitigation options: (1) interim local generic cups with stickers, pending brand approval; (2) expedited air freight for 30% of the order. Decision needed by [date]. No customer-facing impact expected if Option 1 is approved within 48 hours.

**Franchisee version:**
> Thank you for your patience as we finalize the preparations for your store opening. We want to be transparent with you: there is a three-week delay in the delivery of branded packaging materials due to a shipping issue on our end. We are actively working on a solution and will have a confirmed update for you by [date]. Your opening timeline will be adjusted accordingly, and we will make sure you are fully supported through this. We apologize for any inconvenience this causes.

**Legal counsel version:**
> Context: Branded cup supplier (Supplier Name, contract dated X) has notified a 21-day delivery delay due to customs clearance at [port]. This affects two franchise openings scheduled for [dates] in [locations].
> Question 1: Does this delay trigger any obligation notification requirements under the franchise agreements with [Franchisee A] and [Franchisee B]?
> Question 2: Does the supply agreement include a force majeure provision that covers customs delays, and if so, what notice is required?
> Agreements attached. Response needed by [date].

**Government version (if permit extension needed):**
> Dear [Inspector / Officer Name], Re: Food Business License Application — [Store Name], [Address]
> We are writing to respectfully request a 21-day extension to the permit activation deadline referenced above. A logistics delay in the delivery of required equipment has affected our opening timeline. All other compliance requirements have been met. We remain committed to full compliance and would be grateful for your consideration of this request. Please feel free to contact [name, title, contact details] with any questions.
