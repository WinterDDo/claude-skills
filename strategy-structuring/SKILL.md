# Strategy Structuring

## Overview

Clear strategic thinking is not about using frameworks — it is about knowing what matters, why it matters, and what to do about it. Every word in a strategy document should earn its place. No filler, no hedging, no vague optimism.

This skill structures thinking and output for strategic communications at MIXUE Americas — from one-page upward reports to full market entry proposals.

## The Only Rule That Matters

**Every sentence must pass the So What test.**

Before including any sentence, ask: "So what? What decision does this inform? What action does it drive?"

If you cannot answer that question, cut the sentence.

## Core Frameworks

### 1. Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto)

Structure every argument top-down:

```
CONCLUSION (state it first)
  Supporting point 1
    Evidence / data
  Supporting point 2
    Evidence / data
  Supporting point 3
    Evidence / data
```

Never make the reader wait for the conclusion. The conclusion is not a reward for reading — it is the reason to read.

**Wrong:** "We studied the market for three months. We spoke to 40 potential franchisees. We analyzed competitor performance. We reviewed regulatory requirements. Based on all of this, we believe Mexico City is the right entry point."

**Right:** "Mexico City is the right entry point. [Then: evidence, data, context]"

### 2. MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)

When listing options, risks, or factors:
- **Mutually exclusive:** No overlap between items. Each point covers distinct ground.
- **Collectively exhaustive:** Together, the items cover everything relevant. Nothing important is left out.

Test: If two bullet points could be merged without losing meaning, they are not MECE. If the list could be missing a whole category, it is not exhaustive.

### 3. Issue Tree

Use when a problem feels overwhelming or unclear. Break it into sub-questions until each branch is answerable.

```
Main question: Should MIXUE enter Colombia in 2025?
  ├── Is the market opportunity real?
  │     ├── Is there sufficient demand? (demographics, beverage culture)
  │     └── Is the competitive landscape manageable?
  ├── Can we operate profitably?
  │     ├── Do the unit economics work? (CAPEX, EBITDA, payback)
  │     └── Is the supply chain viable?
  └── Are we ready to execute?
        ├── Do we have the legal and compliance infrastructure?
        └── Do we have the right local partner or team?
```

Answer each branch with facts. The overall answer emerges from the branches.

### 4. Long-Range Perspective Check

Before finalizing any strategic recommendation, ask:
- What does this look like in 3 years? In 5 years?
- What trend is this riding — and will that trend still be there?
- What are we optimizing for today that might become a liability later?
- What would we regret not doing if we look back in five years?

This is not about predicting the future. It is about pressure-testing whether today's decision accounts for tomorrow's reality.

## Output Templates

### Upward Report (One Page, HQ or Senior Leadership)

```
[TOPIC] — [REGION / MARKET] — [DATE]

SITUATION
[One sentence: what is the current state of affairs]

KEY FINDING
[One to two sentences: the most important insight, with a number if possible]

RECOMMENDATION
[One sentence: what the Americas BD department proposes to do]

RATIONALE
[Three bullet points maximum: why this is the right call]
• [Reason 1 — data point]
• [Reason 2 — data point]
• [Reason 3 — data point]

RISK
[One sentence: the most important risk + mitigation]

REQUEST
[Specific: what is needed from leadership, by when]
```

### Strategic Proposal (Full Document)

```
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
   Recommendation in one paragraph. Reader should be able to stop here and understand the ask.

2. SITUATION ANALYSIS
   Current state of the market / opportunity. Facts only. No opinion yet.

3. OPPORTUNITY ASSESSMENT
   Why this is worth pursuing. Data, comparables, timing.

4. OPTIONS CONSIDERED
   2 to 3 alternatives evaluated. MECE. Each option with pros, cons, and financial implication.

5. RECOMMENDATION
   Which option and why. Clear logic. Address the strongest counterargument.

6. EXECUTION PLAN
   Key milestones, owners, timeline. Enough detail to be credible, not so much that it becomes a project plan.

7. FINANCIAL SUMMARY
   Key metrics: investment required, expected return, payback period, break-even.

8. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
   Top 3 risks. For each: likelihood, impact, and how we manage it.

9. DECISION REQUESTED
   One specific ask. By when.
```

### Decision Memo (For Internal Alignment)

```
DECISION MEMO
Topic: [One-line subject]
Date: [Date]
Author: Americas BD Department

BACKGROUND
[2 to 3 sentences. Why are we making this decision now?]

DECISION REQUIRED
[One sentence. Specific and unambiguous.]

OPTIONS
Option A: [Name] — [One sentence description] — [Key trade-off]
Option B: [Name] — [One sentence description] — [Key trade-off]

RECOMMENDATION
[Option X] — [Because: one sentence rationale with the most important supporting fact]

IMPLICATIONS
If approved: [What happens next]
If not approved: [What the consequence of inaction is]

DECISION NEEDED BY: [Date]
```

## Writing Standards for Strategy Documents

**Use numbers.** "Strong demand" means nothing. "Average daily foot traffic of 1,200 pedestrians within 100 meters" means something.

**One idea per sentence.** Long sentences with multiple clauses hide weak thinking. Break them up.

**Active voice.** "The Americas BD department recommends..." not "It is recommended by..."

**No filler openers.** Delete: "In today's rapidly changing landscape..." / "As we navigate an increasingly complex environment..." These add zero information.

**No hedging without substance.** "Results may vary" without specifying the range is not risk disclosure — it is noise. Either quantify the risk or describe the scenario that drives it.

**Honest about uncertainty.** When you do not know something, say so. "We estimate X based on [assumption]; actual figures require validation" is stronger than a confident number with no basis.

## Tone in Strategic Documents

Strategy is not marketing. It is not a sales pitch to leadership. It is honest analysis with a clear recommendation.

- Do not oversell. Present the realistic case, not the optimistic one.
- Do not understate risks to make the recommendation look cleaner.
- Show that you have thought about what could go wrong and have a response.
- Be confident in the recommendation, humble about the uncertainty.

The best strategic documents are the ones where leadership feels they understand the situation better after reading them — and trust the team that wrote them.
