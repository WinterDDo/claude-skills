# Negotiation Preparation Framework

## Overview

Structured preparation for any negotiation MIXUE's Americas BD department enters. The goal is to walk in with clarity on what matters, what is flexible, and where the line is — so decisions are made from principle, not pressure.

## Non-Negotiable Foundation: MIXUE's Values

Before any tactic or strategy, these values define the boundary of every negotiation MIXUE participates in. They are not a constraint — they are a strength.

**Integrity, honesty, and trustworthiness are always non-negotiable.**

In practice, this means:
- Never make a commitment you cannot keep, even if it costs the deal
- Never misrepresent facts about the product, the business, or the financial projections
- Never use pressure tactics that exploit the other party's vulnerability or lack of information
- If you discover information that changes the terms significantly, disclose it

These principles apply regardless of how the other side behaves. MIXUE's reputation is built deal by deal. A deal won through deception is a liability, not an asset.

If any negotiation strategy suggested by Claude conflicts with these values, flag it immediately and propose an alternative.

## Pre-Negotiation Preparation Checklist

Complete this before every significant negotiation.

### Understand Yourself
- [ ] What is my primary objective in this negotiation?
- [ ] What is the minimum outcome I can accept? (Walk-away point)
- [ ] What can I offer that the other party values?
- [ ] What is my BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)? If this deal falls through, what do I do?
- [ ] What is my timeline? Am I under time pressure, and does the other party know this?
- [ ] What is my red line? (Values-based limits, not just commercial limits)

### Understand the Other Party
- [ ] What is their primary objective?
- [ ] What pressures are they under (financial, timeline, competitive)?
- [ ] What is their BATNA? What happens to them if this deal does not close?
- [ ] Who is the actual decision-maker? Am I talking to the right person?
- [ ] What do they know about MIXUE, and what might they misunderstand?

### Define Your Position Range
| Issue | Ideal Outcome | Acceptable Range | Walk-Away Point |
|---|---|---|---|
| [e.g., Royalty rate] | 6% | 5% to 7% | Above 8% |
| [e.g., Territory exclusivity] | City-exclusive | Zip code cluster | No exclusivity at all |
| [e.g., Lease term] | 3 years | 3 to 5 years | Above 7 years without exit clause |

## Negotiation Types and Specific Considerations

### Franchise Agreement Negotiation
Key issues: royalty rate, territory size and exclusivity, renewal conditions, termination rights, training and support obligations, sub-franchising rights

What to protect:
- Brand standards and quality control rights — these are non-negotiable for any franchise system
- Right to terminate for cause without excessive notice periods
- Limitation of franchisor liability for franchisee's employee or operational issues

What to be flexible on:
- Phased territory development milestones (especially for new markets)
- Initial training location and format
- Local adaptation of marketing materials

### Commercial Lease Negotiation
Key issues: rent amount, rent escalation clause, lease term, fit-out contribution, break clause, exclusivity of use, renewal option

What to protect:
- A break clause after Year 2 or 3 for new markets (you need an exit if the location does not perform)
- Cap on annual rent escalation (link to CPI, not uncapped)
- Landlord's responsibility for structural repairs

What to be flexible on:
- Lease term length if break clause is secured
- Modest rent increases in exchange for longer exclusivity

### Government and Regulatory Engagement
This is not a traditional negotiation — it is a relationship-building exercise with an authority figure.

- Be patient. Government timelines are not business timelines.
- Bring documentation. Every claim should be backed by paper.
- Never express frustration in the meeting. Follow up in writing.
- Find out the process before trying to accelerate it.
- Build relationships at the working level, not just the top level.

### Supplier and Partner Negotiation
Key issues: price, minimum order quantity, exclusivity, payment terms, quality standards, delivery timeline, dispute resolution

What to protect:
- Right to audit quality and inspect facilities
- Clear penalty or remedy clauses for delivery failure
- No automatic price increase without notice and consent

## Cross-Cultural Negotiation Styles Across the Americas

The same opening offer reads completely differently depending on the culture. Understanding this prevents misreading normal behavior as bad faith.

### North America

**United States**
- Direct, task-oriented, time-conscious
- Get to the point quickly; small talk is brief
- Written contracts are taken very seriously
- Silence during negotiation often means they are thinking, not rejecting
- Expect counteroffers quickly; slow responses can signal loss of interest

**Canada**
- Similar to US but more consensus-oriented and less aggressive
- Building rapport matters more than in the US
- Avoid anything that feels like pressure; it creates resistance

### Mexico and Central America
- Relationship must be established before business is discussed seriously
- First meetings are often social; pushing for a decision too early is seen as disrespectful
- "Yes" can mean "I understand" rather than "I agree" — always confirm in writing
- Hierarchy matters; the senior person in the room has final authority
- Punctuality norms vary; plan for some flexibility in meeting starts

### Caribbean
- Slower pace is cultural, not a signal of disinterest
- Trust is built through personal connection and follow-through over time
- Avoid a transactional approach in early stages
- Reference networks matter — a shared contact can accelerate trust significantly

### South America

**Brazil**
- Warm, relationship-first culture; starting with small talk is expected and genuine
- Negotiations can be emotionally expressive — this is normal, not a warning sign
- Brazilians negotiate hard on price and terms but respect directness and honesty
- Written contracts matter but the personal relationship often determines whether they are actually followed
- Do not rush to close; let the relationship develop

**Argentina**
- Strong national pride; never position MIXUE as superior to local brands
- Argentinians are well-educated and will push back on any weak argument — be prepared
- Economic instability makes them sensitive to pricing and currency terms
- Build trust slowly; skepticism of foreign partners is common

**Colombia**
- Formal in initial meetings, warms up as trust builds
- Decision-making may involve family or close associates even in business settings
- Security considerations influence location and logistics discussions — be sensitive to this

**Chile**
- Most formal and process-oriented in South America
- Punctuality is taken seriously
- Expect thorough due diligence before any commitment
- Conservative in style; avoid aggressive or high-pressure approaches

**Peru, Ecuador, Bolivia**
- Personal relationships are central
- Patience is essential; decisions take longer than in North America
- Price sensitivity is high; respect for local business practices matters

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Reality |
|---|---|
| Making the first concession too quickly | It signals your initial position was not serious and invites further pressure |
| Negotiating against yourself | Do not offer a better deal before they have rejected your current one |
| Confusing silence with rejection | In many cultures, silence means consideration, not no |
| Making a promise to close the deal that you cannot keep | One broken promise can end the entire relationship |
| Focusing only on price | Most deals have 5 to 10 variables; movement on non-price terms often unlocks stuck negotiations |
| Forgetting the relationship after closing | In Latin America especially, the deal is the beginning, not the end |
